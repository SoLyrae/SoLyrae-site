What is Solana? A plain-language introduction

Guide · Nov 13, 2026 · SoLyrae

If you are thinking of staking SOL, it helps to know what you are supporting. This is the short version, without jargon.

A shared ledger that nobody owns

Solana is a public blockchain: a record of who owns what, and of the programs that run on it, kept by many independent computers instead of by one company. Anyone can read it, and nobody can change the past by themselves. People use it to send and receive money, to trade, to hold digital assets and to run applications.

What SOL is for

SOL is Solana's native token. It pays the small fees that every transaction needs, and it is the token you stake to help secure the network.

Who runs the network: validators

The computers that keep the ledger are called validators. They check transactions, vote on which blocks are valid and take turns producing new blocks. On the data our explorer uses, there are a bit more than 680 validators with stake today, run by many different companies and individuals.

Why staking exists

Solana uses proof of stake. A validator's voting weight depends on how much SOL is staked to it. Anyone can add to that weight by delegating their SOL to a validator. This makes it costly to attack the network and rewards the people who help secure it. Delegating does not hand your SOL over: it stays in a stake account that only you control.

Time on Solana: slots and epochs

Solana produces a new block roughly every fifth of a second. Blocks are grouped into epochs, and an epoch now lasts around 26 hours. Staking rewards are paid out at the start of each epoch, and stake activates or deactivates at epoch boundaries.
